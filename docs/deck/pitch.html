Problem

Open-channel hydraulics is taught in every civil-engineering program on Earth — and the only general-purpose tool students touch is HEC-RAS: free, immensely capable, and brutally intimidating on day one.

  • Decades of accumulated UI; 30+ dialogs to compute one profile.
  • Windows-only; classrooms split-screen on Macs via Parallels.
  • No built-in guidance — Manning's n, regime choice, boundary condition are all "you should know this".
  • Commercial wrappers exist (CivilGEO, Krey) — priced $1,500 – $8,000 / seat. Not for students.

Result: a generation of engineers who can operate HEC-RAS but cannot read what it is doing. The math gets hidden behind buttons.

Who feels it

  • Students — pay $150 for a textbook they don't read; need to see the math.
  • Instructors — want a tool that shows the standard-step iteration step-by-step, not hides it.
  • Junior engineers — need a $30 personal copy for refreshers between client jobs.
  • Senior PE designers — not our market. They already have, and need, validated HEC-RAS.
Problem2 / 12

Solution

A native desktop app (macOS / Windows / Linux) that runs the same 1-D steady-flow math as HEC-RAS — Manning, conveyance, critical depth, standard-step — but exposes every step:

  • Companion wizard — explains Froude, regime, roughness in one click; flags shallow depths and near-critical Froude after every run.
  • Live cross-section editor — edit a ground point, see the wetted area recompute under your cursor.
  • Scripting API — Goodell-style automation, runs on Mac/Linux (real HEC-RAS Controller is Windows-COM only).
  • JSON projects — diff-friendly, email-friendly, gradebook-friendly.

The MIT-licensed engine stays free forever. The packaged app is the paid product: signed installers, updates, support, branding.

Five-section sub-critical profile, solved in 18 ms. Thalweg, water surface, energy grade line, and critical WSE in one chart.

Solution3 / 12

Demo · cross-section editor + auto-flagged Froude

Edit any (station, elevation) point; the plot, wetted area, top width, and hydraulic radius update on every keystroke. After a profile run, the companion's post-run summary highlights:

  • Healthy — all sections converged, regime consistent.
  • Near-critical Froude (|Fr − 1| < 0.05) — flagged with the explanation of why mixed-flow analysis would be safer.
  • Velocity > 3 m/s — likely bank erosion; suggests rip-rap or grass-lining design pages.

Every flag is a deterministic rule with a textbook citation, not a language-model hallucination.

Why now

  • HEC-RAS 6.x shipped its first cross-platform-friendly push (Mac/Linux via container) — Mac-first classrooms are looking for native alternatives.
  • App-store small-business rates (15%) across Apple and Microsoft make sub-$50 desktop software economically viable again.
  • Climate-adaptation curriculum mandates added at ABET-accredited programs since 2024 increased hydraulics teaching load — instructors are hunting for friendlier tools.
  • Python ubiquity in CE programs means our scripting API has a class of users it didn't five years ago.

What is genuinely new

No competitor ships a $30, Mac-native, "explain the math at every step" hydraulics app. The space exists because:

  • HEC-RAS is so dominant nobody bothered to teach through it.
  • Pricing the niche correctly (textbook-chapter, not engineering-tool) is contrarian — easy to dismiss, hard to compete with from above.

Musk-mode10 / 12

Risks & how we address them

Trademark / naming

USACE owns "HEC-RAS"; selling a paid product with "Hecras" in the name invites store rejection.

Mitigation: rename before launch (one constant in mirrorz/__init__.py); candidates: ChannelMirror, OpenReach, RiverStep, FlowProfile Studio.

Engineering liability

If marketed for design (not learning) we inherit professional- liability costs no $30 tool can carry.

Mitigation: EULA + in-app banner already state "educational, not for regulatory or life-safety engineering." Marketing copy uses verbs learn / explore.

Free competition (HEC-RAS itself)

Our competition is literally free. Why pay?

Mitigation: we don't try to replace HEC-RAS. We sell understanding it — and a clean Mac/Linux experience HEC-RAS does not provide.

Niche ceiling

Honest cap is single-digit thousands of paid users.

Mitigation: kept costs near zero (no staff, no servers, no ads). Profitable at small scale.

The ask

This is a self-funded, indie-scale project. The right asks are not money:

  • An introduction to a hydraulics professor willing to pilot the classroom tier in fall semester.
  • A boost when the Show-HN goes live.
  • A code review on the standard-step solver from any PE who teaches hydraulics — accuracy is the entire moat.
  • Feedback on the renamed brand shortlist.

If — and only if — Musk-mode Q4 milestones hit, we'd consider a small angel round (≤$100k) to fund the storm-sewer vertical expansion. Today's deck does not ask for it.
